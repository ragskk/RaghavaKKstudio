Calling All Gods

The capacity to stay standing in a fracturing world.

Two paintings, each at the scale of Guernica, stand side by side as one unbroken wall. From across the room they read as devotion. Walk closer and the gods start to misbehave: myths and cartoons, saints and villains, the faces nobody admits to reaching for.

VenueParsons School of Design, NY Date10 December 2026 ScaleTwo paintings · 12.5 × 25 ft each AlsoSeven gods as toys · one book
§ 01 · The premise

Everyone alive right now is reaching for the most ridiculous and magnificent collection of gods imaginable, just to get through this century.

Not the gods we believe in. The gods we use.

Artist statement

What kind of human being can live in a fractured world without retreating into simplistic answers?

Calling All Gods began as a personal search after my certainty collapsed. Over four years, these paintings became an attempt to answer a larger question: what kind of human being can live in a fractured world without retreating into simplistic answers?

Drawing from myth, history, memory and lived experience, the work does not seek to reconcile contradictions, but to enlarge the space that can hold them. The paintings ask whether maturity is not the elimination of complexity, but the capacity to remain fully present within it.

Raghava KK · Bengaluru · 2026

Exhibition statement

At a moment when public life rewards certainty, polarization and reduction, Calling All Gods offers a radically different proposition. Rather than asking viewers to choose between tradition and modernity, faith and doubt, or East and West, the exhibition proposes that our greatest challenge is to become large enough to hold these tensions without collapsing them into a single answer.

In doing so, it reframes painting not as an escape from contemporary life, but as a practice for inhabiting complexity.

§ 02 · The two paintings

One body. One survivor. Side by side.

Two canvases, each at the scale of Picasso's Guernica. Acrylic on canvas, painted across four years, 2022 to 2026.

Plate i

La Liberté de Guernica

A woman stands on the ruins of Guernica. The ground beneath her is broken. Beside her, a double-headed tiger, not a symbol but the actual weight of irreconcilable things that do not leave. She is not riding it, not conquering it. She is standing. They will call her Liberty. That is wrong. She is not free. She is standing, and the difference is everything.

Acrylic on canvas · 12.5 × 25 ft · 2022–2026

Plate ii

Vishwaroopa

The universal form from the Bhagavad Gita, one body holding everything that cannot be held together. Gods, ideologies, time, violence, contradiction, all at once. Arjuna once knelt at its feet. He has been removed. The figure the painting needs is the person standing in front of it now.

Acrylic on canvas · 12.5 × 25 ft · 2022–2026

§ 03 · The pantheon

The gods you can hold.

Not the gods anyone believes in. The gods we actually use, the ones with no temple and no name, brought down off the canvas and turned into toys.

Seven of the gods step down from the wall and become playthings, small enough to hold. A pig that points a finger. A bald eagle. An infant crowned as a patron saint. A god of doomscrolling. A god of patterns and tools. Cast as toys, they are easier to admit to. You use them too. You just haven't said it out loud.

§ 04 · The book

The personal narrative, as a zine.

The measured version is on the wall. The raw one, the desert, the collapse, the four years, is in the book.

Calling All Gods began in a desert, on a satellite phone, at the end of a marriage. The full first-person account behind the paintings lives in a zine that leads the show. Pluck it off the shelf to read it; the spreads turn.
