# PASSPORT

Raghava KK x Ara Lumiere
Featuring Akkiamma
2026

A collaborative art project by Raghava KK and acid attack survivor Akkiamma, examining the politics of identity through portraiture.

## Artist statement

This body of work begins with a simple proposition: a passport photograph is meant to be the most objective image of a person. It functions as an official record of identity, reducing an individual to a face that can be verified, catalogued and recognized.

The project asks what happens when that certainty is dismantled.

Each collaboration begins with a genuine passport photograph taken in a public passport studio. From this single image, Raghava KK creates multiple interpretations through digital and physical interventions. These altered portraits are deliberately subjective. They do not attempt to reveal the sitter's "true self." Instead, they acknowledge that every act of looking is shaped by memory, assumption, culture and personal bias.

The participant is then invited to choose among these interpretations and paint directly onto them.

Before any painting begins, the conversation centres on courage rather than aesthetics. The invitation is not to create beautiful paintings, but to make fearless marks. The photograph is no longer treated as something that must be preserved. It becomes something that can be questioned, interrupted and reclaimed.

The first collaboration in the series was created with Akkiamma, an acid attack survivor. During the workshop, she initially resisted painting over the photographs, feeling that she was damaging them. Through conversation and experimentation, that hesitation gradually shifted. The discussion moved away from the idea of defacement and towards the possibility of authorship. What resembled her was not necessarily her. The image could be challenged, altered and claimed as her own.

Another acid attack survivor and art enthusiast, Maria, joined the workshop to accompany Akkiamma and participate in the conversations surrounding the process.

The finished works are collaborations in the fullest sense. Each portrait contains three distinct layers of authorship: an institutional identity created through the passport photograph, an artistic interpretation created by Raghava KK and a final intervention by Akkiamma herself. None of these layers replaces the others. Instead, they remain visible, allowing identity to exist as something negotiated rather than fixed.

This project is not about restoring an original face or documenting trauma. It is about examining who has the authority to construct identity in the first place.

If a passport can declare who we are, if society continually projects its own readings onto us, then perhaps the final act belongs to the individual who chooses to draw across the image and say, "I am more than how I am seen."

## Process

1. The official photo. The participant takes a genuine passport photograph in a public passport studio.

2. The manipulated image. Raghava creates multiple interpretations of that single image through digital (AI) and physical intervention, including pouring acid onto some prints and re-photographing them, distorting and reworking the portrait. The interpretations are printed on photographic paper.

3. The workshop. In a workshop, Raghava interviews and speaks with the participant, centering the conversation on courage rather than aesthetics.

4. The reclaiming. The participant paints directly onto the interpretations she chooses, reclaiming the image as an act of authorship and bravery.

## Plates

Fourteen works. Each 22 x 30 in, mixed media on digitally manipulated photograph print on 150 gsm glossy photo paper, 2026.

## Raghava KK bio

Raghava KK is a contemporary artist working across painting, performance, and artificial intelligence, whose work examines identity, portraiture, and the construction of the self. Trained as a cartoonist, he has developed a twenty-five-year practice across traditional and digital media, from the Reimagining History paintings to Guernica for the Siri-ocene (Volte Art Projects, Dubai, 2022; acquired by the Kiran Nadar Museum of Art) and Impossible Bouquet, which opened the India Art Fair in 2023. In 2013 he became the first artist inducted into the National Geographic Explorer network, and he has spoken at TED. He lives and works between New York and Bangalore.

## About Ara Lumiere

Ara Lumiere is a couture fashion house founded by Bengaluru-based entrepreneur and philanthropist Kulsum Shadab Wahab, established under the Hothur Foundation, that works with acid-attack and burn survivors, not as beneficiaries of charity but as designers and makers. Each handcrafted garment is conceived and sewn by the survivors themselves, turning their stories into wearable expressions of recovery and authorship; the brand describes itself as an institution of resilience built on the principle that survivors "do not want sympathy, they want opportunity," and frames its work as rebirth rather than repair. Alongside the atelier, Ara Lumiere provides medical, psychological, and economic support, practices an "eco-gender" ethic of recycling and upcycling materials, and has extended its reach through a ready-to-wear line, Ara Pret. Wahab has also been named the first Indian Women's Empowerment Ambassador for Italy's national chamber of fashion, Camera Nazionale della Moda Italiana.
