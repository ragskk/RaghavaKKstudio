## Ganapati

He doesn't roar.
He returns in silence.
in the curve of a tusk
in the weight of a line remembered by the body
before the mind.

Not the Ganesha of temples.
the one of kitchens.
the one from stories my grandmother told me
when the lights were out.
the one who watched over our messes
not just our prayers
. [source sets a lone period on its own line]
He's been passed down like breath
great-grandfather
father
me
not to be preserved
but lived with
. [source sets a lone period on its own line]
We live in a time that trades Gods for algorithms
ritual for novelty
but i still draw Him
not to perform faith
but to feel it.
not idol

Not ornament
but presence.
He is memory.
He is still here.

Raghava KK, 2025

## About the collection

This series began over twenty years ago, when my father translated a rare, beautiful book written by his grandfather, Neelakanta Shastri, on contemplating Ganesha through Advaitha.

I didn't understand it fully then. But something stayed with me. The feeling. The presence.

Around the same time, my grandmother was telling me stories, some sacred, some mischievous, all full of life. Ganesha became more than a god. He became a thread connecting generations, a shape I could keep returning to, again and again.

I set out to make 108 Ganeshas. Not all at once. Just when they called to me. They've held me through transition, through silence, through reinvention.

Each one is a part of me. It's been more than 7 years since I last made them. Now, 10 new ones have arrived.

This is a personal offering. I'm not trying to modernize tradition. I'm trying to keep it alive in a way that's true to who I am now. If one speaks to you, you'll know.

Raghava KK
