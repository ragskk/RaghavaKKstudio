## Slide 2 — Curation thesis in brief

- Art is Transcendence 
Historically, art has always been an experience of a beyond the ‘everyday self’ (i.e. transcendence)
- Transcendence is dependent on the essence of man being beyond the mechanical and beyond nature. However, the terms man, nature, machine have changed with Darwinism, the discovery of DNA, climate change, and machine learning.

Transcendence has to be redefined
Our definition of transcendence has to change 
 
The experience of Transcendence is mysterium tremendum (terrorizes) fascinams (attracts) 
In religious studies, one of the most influential descriptions of transcendence (Otto) has been of a mystery that is both terrorizing and at the same time, fascinating.

Transcendence for the future has to be based on these four changes in the way we see ourselves
1. We think of man as a machine
2. Machines are becoming behaviorally indistinguishable from humans
3. Mind - Body distinction has been problematized -- man is seen as purely biological
4. Nature is gaining personhood

Art that evokes these new forms of transcendence is art that is relevant for the future

## Slide 4 — Our definition of transcendence has to change

- The first phase of changes in the traditional model involved systematically demoting man and nature to machine
- With Newton Reducing Nature to Machine, Darwinism reducing Man to Nature and the discovery of DNA reducing Man to Machine. This led to art movements like the romantic movement, expressionism, dada, futurism and finally Marcel Duchamp’s aesthetics of recontextualization and its descendants.
- The second phase involves promoting machine and nature to man
- Consciousness of climate change, complexity of economies led to Nature/ System being thought of as the new locus of responsibility and agency. Machines have become our natural environment with the rise of the internet. With the rise of machine learning and artificial intelligence, machines are poised to replace and even outdo man in all spheres of activity.
