## Slide 5

- A NEW ART PRACTICE
- The show brings together pioneering Indian AI artists, artisans and curators who have adopted a radically new aesthetic practice: their art process is created by practice of artificially intelligent(AI) algorithm working closely with traditional Indian artisans and contemporary artists and curators.
- We pioneered the first global gallery exhibition of AI art. However we want to take AI art to the next level where it’s not just about AI but has considerable conceptual depth as well. Aside from the social consequences of AI, we aim to explore the philosophical challenges for the future of aesthetics posed by the very existence of AI art.
- What is AI art?
- The kind of AI algorithm used by the artists, is called a neural network: its design was meant to mimic the activity of biological neurons. To teach a neural network a visual language, the artist does not provide it with any rule or principle; she only exposes it to a large number of examples (called training set) and allows it to draw its conclusions. After this ‘training’, the AI is then ready to start creating the final work.
- The training is by no means a simple process. Aside from the role of creating the conceptual basis of the artwork, it is here that the human artist’s novel practice is situated. This is the art of the statistician; the art of the dataset, of choosing the examples one uses for ‘training’. There are several issues here: how to curate/create the training set, how to impose visual constraints on it, how to adjust how quickly the algorithm learns. And all this is done with a more traditional aesthetic eye on the quality of the work the AI creates. In any case, the putative gap between the ‘creative’ practices of the artist and the supposedly logical practice of the scientist is problematized.

## Slide 6

- IMPACT
- THE DIRECT IMPACT FOR THE BUSINESS WORLD AND THE ARTWORLD
- Our future is AI. We need to confront this. If the machine becomes expert even at creating art, what is left for human artists to do? This is a question and a problem of great consequence for the artists of the future. This show will provide at least one way forward: become cyborg.
- We believe that art trends can be started in India and rooted in Indian aesthetics and we do not need to continue copying Western trends. This exhibition is conceived at something at the cutting edge of global art.
- We live in a time dominated by divisive politics. This show disturbs settled identity categories like caste, gender, etc. with the goal of creating larger awareness of issues that face all of humanity like the rise of AI
- We engage traditional artisans with this art of the future, so we can see AI as a tool to enrich traditional arts rather than something that will displace them

## Slide 12

THE CURATORS

64/1

- 64/1 - A curatorial collective focused on art for the Post-Human age
- 64/1 is an art curation and research collective focused on building public understanding of how artists and artificial intelligence can come together to create art for the post-human age. They have written the first academic document coming out of the Indian art world addressing the ability of contemporary art to help create an experience of a dynamic human-machine relationship. 64/1 was founded by Raghava KK and Karthik Kalyanaraman out of Bangalore, India. Raghava KK is a multidisciplinary artist, TED speaker and TED curator. Karthik Kalyanaraman is a former professor of econometrics at University College London and University of Maryland, College Park.

## Slide 13

THE ARTISTS

Dr. Karthik Kalyanaraman co-founded 64/1 along with his brother Raghava KK. He started by studying Art History but ended up completing his PhD in Econometrics from Harvard University, and was a professor of the same at University College London and University of Maryland, College Park. Dr. Kalyanaraman is also a curator, creator of AI art, and an experience designer.

- Raghava KK was named by CNN as one of the 10 most remarkable people in 2010. He is a multidisciplinary artist and entrepreneur working at the intersection of art, science, technology, education and entrepreneurship.
- Raghava is a National Geographic Explorer and a multiple-time TED speaker.
- He along with his brother Dr. Karthik Kalyanaraman co-founded 64/1, a research collective focused on art for the post human age. Their debut curatorial project Gradient Descent recognized as the first AI Art exhibition in India, and perhaps one of the first serious ones at a gallery, attracted pioneers from 9 countries. Their paper ‘Transcendence for the Siri-ocene’ was published at India’s leading aesthetics journal Critical Collective.
- Raghava and his brother also co-authored the acclaimed board game Shasn.
- Popular Netflix show “The Creative Indians” dedicated an episode on his art and life.

- Harshit Agrawal is a new media artist and human computer interaction (HCI) researcher. Through his artwork, he wants to create experiences for people to explore and express with seemingly distant technologies like artificial intelligence/ machine learning, drones, digital fabrication, sensors, augmented reality and in the process invite people to reflect upon and re-evaluate their relationship with technology.
- He believes we live within a complex sphere of technical processes, and seldom do our experiences make us stop and come face to face with them, sit down and have a conversation with them, become comfortable with them to a level that we start reflecting on how they are truly affecting us.
- As an artist, he wants to raise questions around such influences that technology has on us.

## Slide 14

THE ARTISTS

- SiriDevi Khandavalli works across painting, performance, video, and installation to create visual art that is rooted in traditional Indian techniques and yet transcends cultural specificity to hint at larger commentaries about the materialistic nature of the present. She is best known for her cheeky Chola-like bronzes.
- Other notable works include Eat (2009), a looped video critiquing mindless consumption, and Lucky Lakshmi Dollar Bill (2010), a conceptual work created from US currency that emphasizes the multiple connotations of money across cultures. Seen as a whole, Khandavalli’s diverse body of work is centered on the notion of desire and seeks to answer the question of what is it that moves people and the world.

- Shilo Shiv Suleman is a multi award winning artist, INK fellow and TED speaker with a focus on the intersection of magical realism, art for social change and technology. In most recent years, she’s been engaging with biofeedback technology, and the interaction between the body and art.
- She has created large scale installations that beat with your heart, apps that react to your brainwaves and sculptures that glow with your breath. She’s been featured in a host of documentaries, and been recipient of several international grants.
