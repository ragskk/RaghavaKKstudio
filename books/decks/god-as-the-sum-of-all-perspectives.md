## Slide 2

The Starting Point

01

The Problem with Traditional God

Why These Definitions Fail

I do not understand God as a being, a will, or a moral authority.

God as being — collapses into anthropomorphism, a projection of human qualities onto the infinite

I do not understand God as someone who listens, intervenes, forgives, or plans.

God as will — introduces intention where there may be only structure, preference where there must be neutrality

Those ideas collapse too quickly into comfort, fear, or self-projection. They fail under pressure.

God as moral authority — becomes a tool for power, a way to justify human systems under divine mandate

God as intervener — creates impossible expectations, then disappointment when the world proceeds according to its own logic

What I am trying to articulate is stricter and less consoling.

## Slide 3

The Core Metaphor

02

The Gyre: Structure Without Resolution

A gyre is not balance. It is not equilibrium.

It is two opposing forces spiraling through each other over time, never canceling, never resolving.

Expands

Contracts

One cone opens outward, reaching toward possibility

The other draws inward, holding to what is known

One dominates, then yields. Meaning, identity, culture, and history do not progress in straight lines. They rotate under pressure.

The gyre is not a symbol of everything. It is a constraint that appears wherever contradiction must persist without collapse.

## Slide 5

Scales of the Same Problem

04

From Personal to Collective

If this is true at the scale of a person, it is also true at the scale of a society.

The Same Structure

Tradition ↔ Revolution

Not opposites that defeat each other, but paired forces rotating through time.

Individual

Society

What hardens eventually fractures

- Memory vs. Possibility
- Order vs. Disruption
- Belonging vs. Truth

- Tradition vs. Revolution
- Structure vs. Change
- Continuity vs. Transformation

Individuals and societies are not different kinds of things.

They are different scales of the same problem.

## Slide 6

The Precise Definition

05

God as Totality

God is not inside the gyre.

God is not one side of it.

God is not the point of intersection.

God is the totality of all gyres taken together.

The complete, unresolved simultaneity of all perspectives.

No Standpoint

No Preference

No Intention

Any standpoint would exclude another and collapse the definition

Any preference would privilege one cone over the other

Intention requires exclusion, which God cannot have

This definition is precise enough to be uncomfortable.

## Slide 7

What God Cannot Do

06

God as Limit Condition

God Cannot...

Choose

A Limit Condition

Choosing requires preferring one possibility over another

Act

God is closer to a limit condition than an entity.

Acting requires having a standpoint from which to act

Intervene

Intervening requires believing some states are better than others

The boundary within which all partial knowing becomes possible, and beyond which nothing coherent can be said.

God is not conscious in the way humans are conscious, because consciousness requires exclusion, attention, and foreground.

This explains why God is always unknowable without mysticism.

## Slide 8

Theological Implications

07

Why Religions Fragment

Any claim to know God fully is simply a local perspective mistaking itself for the whole.

Fragment

Harden

Reverse

Each perspective crystallizes into doctrine, believing itself complete

Defensive structures form to protect the partial truth from dissolution

The gyre turns, and what was orthodoxy becomes heresy

Theology is not revelation from above.

Theology is autobiography under pressure.

## Slide 9

The Hardest Implication

08

Suffering Without Redemption

What Is Real

This model does not solve suffering.

Loss is real.

It refuses to.

Death is final at the level that matters.

When a perspective ends, nothing is cosmically restored.

Suffering is not redeemed elsewhere.

It is not justified by a larger plan.

It is what constraint feels like from the inside.

A crossing simply ceases.

## Slide 10

Why This Is Not Nihilism

09

Ethics Without Absolution

Meaning does not come from cosmic endorsement.

Choice Remains Real

It comes from responsibility at the point of intersection.

But bounded.

I do not choose outcomes in the universe

Ethics Is Not...

I choose which tension resolves through this body

Obedience to God.

It is care for the viability of future perspectives.

That choice matters precisely because it is local and irreversible.

Cruelty

Care

This is not a comforting God. It does not watch over me. It does not protect me. It does not guarantee justice.

Wrong because it narrows the field

Right because it keeps the gyre open

## Slide 11

Even My Parents Deferred

Certainty Inherited

Karthik Made It Visible

That mattered. If my parents could be wrong, God couldn't be.

Not discovered. You didn't argue with it. You aligned or you stayed quiet.

He knew things: scripture, philosophy, ritual. He spoke with confidence.

He wasn't trying to dominate. But when someone seems sure, everyone else feels stupid by comparison. That shaped me more than I admit.

## Slide 12

The Breaking Point

11

Education and Collapse

God didn't fade.

God failed.

Even after God collapsed intellectually, the longing didn't.

## Slide 14

The Turning Point

13

William James as Permission

What James Offered

Permission

He didn't argue for God

He took experience seriously

To take my own experience seriously without needing to justify it to anyone else.

He made it clear that yearning itself mattered

That I hadn't rejected God because I didn't care, but because my mind refused dishonesty while my body kept reaching.

James gave me language for what I was feeling: that the will to believe could be legitimate, that yearning could be a form of knowledge.
