How Figure & Edges were made.

One journey, two series. The Figurative Series was built first, on paper and canvas; the Edges Series broke out of its margins. February to August 2020, Bengaluru. The exercises, the journals, the critiques, and the works, in order, as they happened. On 6 July the page turns dark, because the canvases did.

PrologueThe condition

Two failures, early 2020.

Outside: March 2020. Commissioned work worth half a million dollars, cancelled in one call. Weeks later, lockdown.

Inside, the real problem: the work had become formulaic. A recognisable composition, a digital-to-canvas pipeline that ran reliably without its maker. Even my unpredictability became predictable. The work no longer surprised the person making it.

The cancellations did not cause the reinvention. They removed the excuse not to. The formula was already the problem. Also in the house: his brother, the art critic Karthik Kalyanaraman, back in Bengaluru, starting over at the same time.

The apparatus64/1

Before any painting: 64/1.

An artist alone cannot escape his own formula. So the first step was a structure of three people, formalised with Karthik as 64/1. Its objective: examine the history of the work, critique the current body, and arrive, through the process itself, at something more original.

Karthik Kalyanaraman
The Critic
Interrogates the history of the work. Sets the exercises. Refuses. In the artist's word, midwifes the series.
Raghava KK

Mar 2020Whiteboard
The interrogation

Who is Raghava?

A whiteboard and a question. How I see myself; how others see me; what the practice says about me regardless of what I intend.

It ended in three words: play, pain, the construction of identity. Everything after this is tested against them. The aesthetic is built to carry them.

Mar 4–11Guernica
The ancestor

Raghava vs Picasso.

Guernica, printed large in black and white and laid on the floor. First its characteristics; then the harder question: where, precisely, is the disagreement? The exercises that follow are built from the answer.

Exercises on paper.

Mar 13First oils
The medium

First oils. Wanted for twenty years, avoided for twenty years.

Oil is what the masters used; it demands proficiency. No art school, a career built in acrylic and pixels. Impasto both exciting and frightening. That was the reason to choose it. These are the first oil paintings of his life.

Exercise · March 13Create four works using any four colours: an abstract work that shows nihilism, play and self-construction.

No figure permitted. Identify which marks worked; keep only those. The kept marks become the style vocabulary, built before it is allowed to describe anything.

Mar 19Characters

The new style follows a form.

The prompt: use the newly created style to follow a form. Mickey, the Devil, Cuphead, rebuilt in the new vocabulary. The pencil beside the oil shows the distance travelled.

Apr 21Print

Painting over the print.

The digital Guernica composition, the old pipeline’s product, printed archivally on canvas, then painted over by hand in the new language. The old way underneath, the new way on top.

May 2020The loop
The loop

Question → incubation → attempt → silence → critique.

The working rhythm, February to August: Karthik poses a question. Days of journaling, no painting yet. Then the attempt. Then silence: nobody judges fresh work. Then the critique. Then the next question.

The delays are the point: they break the reflex that produces formula. And nobody steers toward an outcome. The loop has questions and a filter, but no destination.

Exercise · May 12Use the new style to create a large work. Look out for interesting marks & happenings.
Jun 2020Figurative
The series

The Figurative Series.

Canvases weekly through June and early July, characters carrying the three words. The palette is written down on June 25, after it has proved itself. From the journals: formula → lazy → broken. Every work treated as an individual.

From the pages

6 July 2020

The first Edges work.

A small diptych, 18 × 11 inches each. Unplanned. The impasto that had been building along the edges of the figurative canvases stops being a side-effect and becomes the subject. The edge, the ridge of paint, the side of the object, the part a photograph cannot capture, becomes the work. The second series is born.

Jul 6The accident
The accident

The First Diptych.

The journals had already written the demand: move away from painting as image, toward painting as object. An edge cannot be reproduced. It has to be seen in person.

Jul 7Approval

The first large work. Guruji approves.

Next day: the first large Edges canvas, 6 × 4 feet, marks on black. The critic’s reaction, kept in the record: this is fresh, this is original, and the maker surprising himself again, which is what the whole process was for.

Jul 19–24Family

The family enters the work.

The subjects come from inside the house: Jayamma, a triptych. Mother’s work. Rudra. Aadya. Anaga. Jaya. Play, pain, and the construction of identity, painted as the people who constructed his.

Jul 20Research

How am I different?

Two research lists, eighteen names from a friend, nine of the maker’s own, Auerbach to Joan Mitchell to Altmejd, each examined in writing against one question. The notes are blunt: more abstract than Auerbach. A strong body connection to Altmejd. Schulnik has painted on edges, but not as the main feature.

Jul 31Writing

From the pages

Aug 3–6Late works

Bios vs Zoe · The Positive · The Negative.

The last recorded canvases: Bios vs Zoe, and a diptych: The Positive, The Negative. Then, August 6: one day of sorting. Top candidates, secondary works, the rest let go.

At one point I felt: I have arrived at something. Right. Full stop.

The maker’s own verdict, after six months of a process built to keep him honest.
EpilogueAfter the full stop

After the series.

Selection: one day, every work sorted. Display: platforms, inclines, ramps, worked out on paper, because the works read from all sides. The catalogue: made after the series was finished. The record follows the work; it does not lead it.

The journals had already asked the question this page answers: “When the film world has accommodated making-of documentaries as films, why hasn’t the process of art become as mainstream? Both are scared to reveal their tricks. It may lose value then.”

CodaOne series, one process

This process belongs to Figure & Edges alone.

Nothing here is a method. The whiteboard, the critic, the delays, the black ground: these were invented for this journey and end with it. Each new series begins by building its own process.
