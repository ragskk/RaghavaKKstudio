№ 02Process · Toy Trojan · 2020

How the Toy Trojan was made.

A record of one object. The main frame had to come out of a single piece of wood, so the work began in a forest rather than a studio: a search that ran for months, a three-hundred-year-old mahogany, a ritual asking permission before the cut, and a three-kilometre walk out on foot. Only then the carving, and then the surface, into which the whole present was written.

PrologueThe premise

A gift with something inside it.

The Trojan horse is the oldest argument about form: an object offered as one thing while carrying another. This sculpture takes the argument literally. A horse, at the scale of a monument, finished to read as a toy, and packed, on its surface and through its opened body, with everything a toy is supposed to keep out.

That decision set the constraints. A toy is one continuous moulded thing, so the frame could not be assembled from parts: it had to be cut from a single log. And a single log large enough meant a tree old enough to be worth asking permission of.

2019The search
The material

One tree, and three to six months to find it.

A team of thirty went into the forest with government permission and searched for a single piece of wood large enough to carve the main frame. The hunt ran between three and six months. What they found was a three-hundred-year-old mahogany.

Getting it out was its own operation. There was no vehicle access, so the log was carried roughly three kilometres on foot across a damp paddy field: around sixteen people to a log.

2019Permission
The ritual

Asking the tree first.

Before the cut, a ceremony: a ritual to Mother Nature asking permission to take the tree, and asking that the wisdom and knowledge of three hundred years be preserved in what the wood becomes. Nothing about this is decorative. It is the first instruction the material receives.

Dec 2019Carving
The form

A crude horse, cut out of the log.

The first pass is subtraction only: the rearing form pulled out of the trunk in the roughest possible terms, no detail permitted yet. Then sanding, until the whole surface is smooth enough to be drawn on. The main foundation has to be finished before a single character can be placed on it.

Dec 2019The hands

Not one maker. A workshop.

The carving is done by a workshop, working from the artist's drawings and directly against the maquette. The authorship here is shared and stays visible in the record: thirty in the forest, a bench of carvers after it.

Dec–AprThe surface
The population

Everything the present is made of, carved into one animal.

With the foundation smooth, the surface is drawn on: first digitally over a photograph of the form, then in chalk directly on the wood. Then cut. What goes on is deliberately unsorted: politics and cartoons and video games and gods at the same scale, because that is the order in which they actually arrive.

Political opposites are amalgamated into single heads. Gandhi appears not as a saint but as he appears on a ₹2000 note. A figure flees Picasso's Guernica. Kim Jong-un. Frog Mario from Super Mario Bros. 3. Characters out of Undertale. The Garuda.

Feb 2020The inside
The interior

The body is opened.

The horse is not solid. Its torso is cut through to a ribcage, and inside the ribcage a phoenix is being born from the pith of the stomach. Below that, death: a skull and the skeletal population underneath. Above it all, the crest: the crown jewel of the Trojan, its imagination and its future, and the narrative it is in the middle of making.

This is where the premise pays out. The outside is a toy; the inside is a birth and an underworld, at the same time, in the same log.

2020The skin

Painted to read as a toy.

The wood is primed dark first, so that the bright hue laid over it carries rather than sinks. The top half is then painted to read as a toy, and the lower half left as wood.

The workFinished

The Toy Trojan, finished.

Hand-carved mahogany, 78 by 30 by 30 inches, standing on a base cut from the same tree. Three hundred years of growth, months of searching, and a workshop of hands, resolved into one object.

ViewsFront, rear, base
CodaOne work, one process

This process belongs to the Toy Trojan alone.

A search party in a forest. A permission ritual. Government paperwork. Sixteen people and a paddy field. A carving workshop working from a maquette. A surface written like a feed and cut by hand. None of this is a method, and none of it repeats: the next work will require its own impossible logistics, invented for it.

What survives is the constraint that produced all of it, one frame, one piece of wood, and the fact that a decision made about form at the beginning turned into a season of work in a forest three thousand kilometres from the studio.
