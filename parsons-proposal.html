Calling All Gods.

In a fragmenting world, we do not only need to feel free, or seen, or right. We need to feel held, too. Two monumental paintings, four years in the making, mounted as a three-month exhibition, and a proposal for what holding can look like inside an institution.

§ 1Why this show, now

The world is fragmenting. War, ideology, technology, ecology, identity. The structures people used to lean on are coming apart faster than the language that names them. Culture's standard response is to offer healing, return, rescue. Most of what is breaking will not return. The honest question is no longer how to put the old forms back together. It is what holds the fragments while another form emerges.

A person, a body, a self, a world can lose its old form without disappearing. Something has to hold the fragments in relation long enough for the next form to take. That holding is not therapy. It is architecture. It is what an institution, a community, a painting, or a god can do. And what, in this moment, is conspicuously failing.

That is why this show is necessary now. In a fragmenting world we do not only need to feel free, or seen, or right. We need to feel held, too. Parsons is the right room because a school is one of the few institutions still in a position to demonstrate what holding looks like at scale, over time.

Not rescued.Not healed.Not made whole.Held.

"Undone and held" is architectural language, not the language of recovery. The work does not claim the breaking is a stage on the way to something better. It claims that holding is a condition, and that without it nothing else continues.

The container, expanded

The war and shipping realities pushed the original July date into a longer December–March window. The longer window turned out to be the right architecture for the argument: the show needs duration, not just delivery. It also allowed the second monumental work to be completed in parallel, so the exhibition now reads as a dialogue between two paintings made through the same era of collapse and persistence, rather than a single culmination.

The exhibition concludes with a broader institutional proposal: Reimagining Our Relationship. A 3–5 year intervention extending beyond the exhibition itself.

§ 2Artworks in the Show

Major works

The extended exhibition timeline allowed the completion of the second monumental work, transforming the exhibition from a singular centerpiece into a paired large-scale installation.

Vishwaroopa shows the universe holding everything. La Liberté de Guernica shows what it feels like to live inside that collision as a human being. One painting is the architecture. The other is the inhabitant. Neither is complete without the other.

§ 3Layout

The two monumental paintings form the core spatial experience. Both large works will be installed side-by-side on the wall opposite the gallery entrance.

The remaining gallery space holds supporting materials (journals, sketches, books, textual fragments, and toys), meant to extend the world around the paintings rather than dilute their impact.

The exhibition architecture prioritizes the scale and confrontation of the two paintings first. Everything else reads against the spine

The pair sets the question the rest of the show responds to: what is the architecture that holds a life that has been undone, when no rescue is forthcoming? The other materials in the room read against this pair. They argue, qualify, soften, sharpen. The spine does not bend.

§ 9Final Direction

The exhibition has evolved from a shorter-duration showcase into a longer-form institutional environment. Three shifts define the new shape:

One monumental work → Two monumental works
A static exhibition → An extended social & cultural space
A temporary installation → A possible long-term intervention
Reimagining Our Relationship. A proposal for a 3 – 5 year continuation beyond the exhibition itself

The concluding framework opens a longer conversation with Parsons and The New School, about what an artist's residence inside an institution can look like, when the artist's project is structural rather than therapeutic, and the institution is willing to be held alongside the work.
