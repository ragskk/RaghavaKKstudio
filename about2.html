Folio · AboutShort bio

An artist working between gods and algorithms.

Raghava KK is a contemporary artist working between Bengaluru and New York. The practice runs on painting, sculpture, ceramics, books, AI systems, and a stubborn belief in pluralism, the idea that more than one thing can be true at once.

The Practice

The practice runs in two registers. On one side, paint, canvas, clay, hand. On the other, code, sensors, models, performance. They share a single concern, which is the way a culture makes its gods and tests them. What gets to remain holy. What gets retired. Who is allowed to make the next one.

Raghava paints. He sculpts. He builds books, toys, and AI systems. He has given two TED talks. He has spoken at Harvard, in conversation with writers and physicists and one or two former heads of state. He has been collected privately and institutionally across India, the United States, and Europe. The work has been called pluralist for two decades. The work prefers the word practice.

The current focus is Calling All Gods, a solo show opening at Parsons, New York, in December 2026, built around two paintings and the structural language of being undone and held.

Section · 01

Held in conversation with machines.

Brain sensors, projection mapping, generative models, holograms. The studio treats machines the way a 17th-century miniaturist treated apprentices, with affection, suspicion, and a list of instructions.

Section · 02

Held in conversation with people.

The studio's most important infrastructure is a conversation. A small selection from twenty years of them.

Section · 03

Held in public.

Installations and monumental works. The Robin Hood Foundation library, New York. The Guernica series, restaged for the present.
