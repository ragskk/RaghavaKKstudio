FolioAbout · Short bio

Gods from your contradictions.

Raghava KK is a contemporary artist working between Bengaluru and New York. He makes gods out of the contradictions you live but refuse to name, and builds the systems they live in. Painting, sculpture, books, performance, machines.

The claim

29 years in the record1997 → 2026

The practice runs in two registers. On one side, paint, canvas, clay, hand. On the other, code, sensors, models, performance. They share a single concern: the way a culture makes its gods and tests them. What gets to remain holy. What gets retired. Who is allowed to make the next one.

The method is the cartoonist's, and it has not changed since he drew political cartoons for the national dailies at sixteen. Find the contradiction a person is living. Draw it a little larger than they would like. Let the likeness make the argument. The paintings do this to mythology. The machine works do it to the viewer, reading brainwaves back as paint. The toys do it at shelf scale.

India is the inheritance, not the subject. The work has been called pluralist for two decades. The work prefers the word practice.

The current focus is Calling All Gods, a solo exhibition opening at Parsons, New York, in December 2026, built around two paintings and the structural language of being undone and held.

Instruments · № 01 to 08

One inquiry, eight instruments.

Each was picked up when the question outgrew the last one, and kept because it still works. In order of first use. Hover an instrument to see where it enters the record below.

№ 011997 →

Cartoon

Political cartoons for The Times of India, The Asian Age, Deccan Herald and Indian Express, from the age of sixteen. The Lee 'N' Rags Cartoon Academy, 1998, took the method to some ten thousand children across twenty countries. Caricature is still the spine of the practice: Indian-rooted, irreverent, built to disarm before it provokes.

№ 021998 →

Paint

Oil on canvas, the instrument that stayed. First show in 1998, fifteen canvases painted in one night. Thirteen catalogued series since 2011, from The History Series to Gods vs Gods, each with its own process, abandoned when the series ends. Mythology handled as caricature: gods drawn a little larger than they would like.

№ 031999 →

Stage

When Paintings Dance, 1999: watercolour painted onto dancers, the choreography activating the painting, each performance a distinct work. Bangalore, San Francisco, San Jose. Five TED talks since 2010, gathered with the rest of the recordings in one room.

№ 041998 →

Book

Not Allowed to Cry, 1998, was the first. Pop-it, 2011, is the one people remember: an iPad book you shake, and the parents change. Two fathers, two mothers, a mother and a father. The child stays the same. The library holds the artist books since, 33 Million Gods among them.

№ 052010 →

Machine

Brain sensors, projection mapping, generative models, holograms, a robot arm. Paintings that read the viewer's brainwaves back as paint. Fidelio, 2013, with the conductor's EEG driving the projections. The studio treats machines the way a seventeenth-century miniaturist treated apprentices: with affection, suspicion, and a list of instructions.

№ 062010 →

Curating

The INK Fellows program, founded in 2010 and chaired for its first five years. Gradient Descent, Nature Morte, New Delhi: cited by artnet as the first AI-as-art exhibition staged in a gallery. 64/1, the collective co-founded with his twin brother Karthik Kalyanaraman in 2018, is where the criticism and the teaching compound.

№ 072016 →

Systems

Strange Genders, with Harshit Agrawal: a model trained on drawings of men and women, generating every body in between. The Orgasm Project, 2021: brainwaves recorded during orgasm, given form by a model, painted in oil by a machine. 33 Million Gods, a pantheon that keeps counting. The Lab holds the working apps.

№ 082022 →

Toys

Soft Gods: Power Fluff, Retired Gods, The Doomscrollers, Demi-Gs. The pantheon at shelf scale, where a retired god fits in one hand. The Lab keeps the shelf.

1997Political cartoons for The Times of India, The Asian Age, Deccan Herald and Indian Express, at sixteen. First exhibition, Alliance Française de Bangalore, with Cartoonists Unanimous, the collective he co-founded. Sent by the Government of India to the World Trade Congress, Strasbourg; came home across Europe paying in caricatures.
1998Lee 'N' Rags Cartoon Academy founded, inaugurated by Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee. Not Allowed to Cry, first book, Birla House. First painting show, Black Cadillac, Bangalore: fifteen canvases painted in one night.
1999When Paintings Dance premieres at the Taj West End, Bangalore, with Kamal Haasan. Bronx murals, the first public work in the United States. Meets Luigi Ontani in Rome.
2000When Paintings Dance with Velocity Circus and Cirque du Soleil dancers, San Francisco, for the America India Foundation.
2003Robin Hood Foundation Library Initiative, New York: a permanent work for a public-school library, with Pentagram. Morocco: collaboration with Farid Belkahia, cartooning workshops in Essaouira, work enters the collection of the Royal Family.
2005Anthropomorphism: When Paintings Dance restaged, San Jose. Performances at Lincoln Center and London Fashion Week. Collections: Shah Rukh Khan, Amitabh Bachchan, Sachin Tendulkar.
2000sAlbum art for Paul Simon and Erykah Badu, painted first, adapted to the sleeve.
2007Lectures at the École des Beaux-Arts, Nîmes. Live-painting collaborations with the flamenco singer Pepe Linares.
2008Art Musings, Mumbai. Drawn & Quartered; Brooklyn Bound R-Train.

Fidelio, Skylight Music Theatre, Milwaukee: the conductor's EEG driving the projected imagery. National Geographic Emerging Explorer, the first artist named one. Creator-in-residence, Viacom.
