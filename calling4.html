Calling All Gods

The capacity to stay standing in a fracturing world.

Presented at Parsons School of Design · The New School · New York

Installation view · two paintings, one wall

Overview

At a moment when public life rewards certainty, polarization and reduction, Calling All Gods offers a radically different proposition.

Rather than asking viewers to choose between tradition and modernity, faith and doubt, or East and West, the exhibition proposes that our greatest challenge is to become large enough to hold these tensions without collapsing them into a single answer.

In doing so, it reframes painting not as an escape from contemporary life, but as a practice for inhabiting complexity.

The exhibition brings together two monumental paintings, each at the scale of Picasso's Guernica, made over four years; a pantheon of seven gods recast as toys; and a zine carrying the personal narrative behind the work.

Not the gods we believe in. The gods we use.

From the exhibition

The Paintings

Raghava KK
La Liberté de Guernica, 2022–26
Acrylic on canvas, 12.5 × 25 ft (3.8 × 7.6 m)

A woman stands on the ruins of Guernica, a double-headed tiger beside her: not a symbol, but the actual weight of irreconcilable things that do not leave. They will call her Liberty. That is wrong. She is not free. She is standing, and the difference is everything.

Raghava KK
Vishwaroopa, 2022–26
Acrylic on canvas, 12.5 × 25 ft (3.8 × 7.6 m)

The universal form from the Bhagavad Gita: one body holding what cannot be held together. Every god, every form, every contradiction, simultaneously. Arjuna once knelt before it; he has been removed. The painting needs the person standing in front of it right now.

Four years. One wall.

Picasso painted one war, one town, one canvas. These paintings are made for a moment in which every war is visible at once, and no one can look away.

The Pantheon

Seven gods step down from the paintings and become toys.

The gods with no temple and the ones with no name: power, wealth, attention, distraction. Cast as playthings, small enough to hold, they are easier to admit to. Four of the seven turn live below; drag any figure to look at it from all sides.

The Zine

The personal narrative behind the show.

Calling All Gods began in a desert, on a satellite phone, at the end of a marriage. The zine carries the first-person account behind the paintings: the collapse, the calling on everything within reach, and the four years of painting that followed. Forty-nine pages, published by the studio, 2026.

Artist Statement

Calling All Gods began as a personal search after my certainty collapsed. Over four years, these paintings became an attempt to answer a larger question: what kind of human being can live in a fractured world without retreating into simplistic answers?

Drawing from myth, history, memory and lived experience, the work does not seek to reconcile contradictions, but to enlarge the space that can hold them. The paintings ask whether maturity is not the elimination of complexity, but the capacity to remain fully present within it.

Raghava KK · 2026

The Artist

Raghava KK is a contemporary artist working between Bengaluru and New York. Across two decades his practice has moved through painting, sculpture, books, performance and AI systems, held together by a single concern: pluralism as a practice, the idea that more than one thing can be true at once.

He has spoken twice at TED, exhibited across India, the United States and Europe, and is held in private and institutional collections internationally. Calling All Gods is his most sustained body of work to date.
